Writing Skills

Krea’s writing section asks for a reflective response in not more than 300 words. Plan for 4 short paragraphs. Each prompt below has planning notes; the first three also have a full model response (they are the prompts used in the three online tests, so attempt them there first).

Your checklist

Stays on the prompt and within 300 wordsDoes every paragraph answer the prompt? Trim anything that does not.
Reflection, not just narrationAfter describing an event, did you say what it meant to you?
StructureClear opening, 2–3 developed ideas, a closing that lands.
Specific detailA name, a smell, a number – something only you could have written.
LanguageVaried sentences, correct tense, no repeated words, no clichés such as ‘in this fast-paced world’.
Prompt W1
Some mistakes teach us more than any success. Others we would give anything to undo. Reflect on the idea that failure and regret can shape who we become.
Plan and model response
  • Open with one concrete moment (a mistake you made or saw).
  • Say what it cost and what it taught – be specific, not general.
  • Contrast ‘regret that paralyses’ with ‘regret that teaches’.
  • Close by linking the lesson to who you are trying to become.

In Class 9 I copied a friend's science homework. I told myself it was harmless; the teacher spotted it within a minute, and what stung was not the zero but the look of disappointment on my friend's face. He had trusted me with his work.

For weeks I wished I could rewind that morning. Regret, I learned, comes in two kinds. One kind keeps you replaying the moment until it shrinks you; the other kind asks, ‘What will I do differently?’ The first is a prison, the second a teacher.

I chose, slowly, the second. I apologised, redid the assignment, and started a habit I still keep: beginning homework on the day it is given, so that panic never gets to choose for me. Last year, when a classmate asked to copy my notes the night before a test, I said no – and offered to study with him instead. We both passed.

I still would not mind if I could undo that Tuesday. But I have stopped wishing it away, because the person who refuses shortcuts today was made, in part, by the person who took one. Mistakes cannot be rewound, but they can be put to work.

Prompt W2
A place or a smell can carry you back to a moment you wish you could live again. Write about an experience you would happily repeat – and why it matters to you.
Plan and model response
  • Start with the sensory detail (smell, sound, light).
  • Narrate the experience in 2–3 vivid lines, not a list.
  • Ask: why do I want it again – what did it give me?
  • End with what you now do to keep that feeling alive.

The smell of wet earth after the first monsoon shower takes me, at once, to my grandmother's courtyard. I am nine; the ground steams, the neem leaves drip, and she has placed a steel plate of puffed rice and hot tea at the edge of the verandah because, she says, ‘rain tastes better with something to eat’.

Nothing remarkable happened that afternoon. We simply sat. She told me how she crossed a flooded field to school, and I told her about a cartoon. Yet I would give much to repeat it, because it was the last time I did nothing and felt that nothing was enough.

Today my afternoons are timetables. I look forward to holidays the way a runner looks at a finishing line. That afternoon reminds me that happiness was never in the event; it was in the attention. So when it rains now, I switch off my phone for ten minutes, open the window and breathe in. It is not the courtyard, but for ten minutes it is close – and I have learned that repeating a moment sometimes means recreating its stillness, not its setting.

Prompt W3
‘We do not always get a second chance.’ Reflect on a decision that could not be undone and on what you would keep, even if time could be rewound.
Plan and model response
  • Name the decision and why it was final.
  • Show the two sides: what you lost and what you gained.
  • State what you would keep even if you could rewind.
  • Close with a principle for future choices.

Two years ago I left my school's football team to concentrate on exams. The decision was final: the squad that year went on to win the district cup, and my place was filled by someone else.

For a while I felt I had made a poor trade. Every Saturday I heard the cheering from the ground and told myself the results would justify it. Then they did – partly. My marks improved; my friendships on the team faded.

If I could rewind, would I choose differently? I think I would keep the decision but change the way I made it. I decided alone, in one evening, on the basis of fear. A conversation with my coach might have shown me a way to do both. What I would keep is the courage to put a long-term goal ahead of an immediate pleasure; what I would change is the habit of deciding in silence.

That is the rule I use now: for any choice that cannot be undone, I sleep on it and ask one person who knows me well. Second chances are rare, but second opinions are free.

Prompt B1
Technology has made us more connected and more lonely. Reflect on this idea in not more than 300 words.
Plan
  • Define ‘connected’ vs ‘close’.
  • Give one personal or observed example.
  • Weigh both sides honestly.
  • End with a practical habit.
Prompt B2
Describe a small act of kindness that changed your day, and what it taught you about people.
Plan
  • Narrate the act briefly.
  • Explain your feeling before/after.
  • Generalise carefully: what does it show about people?
  • End with how you pass it on.
Prompt B3
‘The best way to predict the future is to create it.’ What does this mean to you?
Plan
  • Interpret the quote in your own words.
  • Give a goal you are working towards.
  • Mention an obstacle and how you respond.
  • Close with a forward-looking line.
Prompt B4
If you could change one rule in your school, what would it be and why?
Plan
  • State the rule and its stated purpose.
  • Argue its drawback with an example.
  • Propose an alternative.
  • Anticipate one objection and answer it.
Prompt B5
A time you were wrong and had to admit it. How did it feel, and what did you do next?
Plan
  • Set the scene briefly.
  • Describe the feeling honestly.
  • Explain the action you took.
  • Say what changed in you.
Prompt B6
Some say talent matters more than hard work; others disagree. Where do you stand?
Plan
  • Take a clear position in the first paragraph.
  • Use one example for each side.
  • Concede a point to the other view.
  • Conclude with your reasoned stance.
Prompt B7
Choose an object that is important to you and write about what it reveals about who you are.
Plan
  • Introduce the object with one sensory detail.
  • Tell its story.
  • Link it to your values.
  • End with what it means for the future.
Prompt B8
Is it better to be a big fish in a small pond or a small fish in a big ocean?
Plan
  • Choose a side early.
  • Support with a personal or familiar example.
  • Acknowledge the cost of your choice.
  • Close with what you want from your own ‘pond’.
Prompt B9
Write about a moment when you felt completely out of your comfort zone.
Plan
  • Place the reader in the moment.
  • Show your thoughts, not just actions.
  • Describe the turning point.
  • Finish with what you carry from it.
