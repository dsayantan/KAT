Krea’s official sample test, solved

The only published KAT material is the Sample Krea Aptitude Test (PDF): 15 multiple-choice questions and one essay prompt. Krea gives no answer key; the solutions below are ours. Two verbal items are ambiguous and are flagged.

Problem-Solving
Question 1 · Official sample
A, B, C, D, E, F and G sit along a wall facing North. C is immediately to the right of D. B sits at an extreme end and has E as a neighbour. G is between E and F. D sits third from one of the ends. Who sits in the middle?
  1. Fanny
  2. Gabriel
  3. Dereck
  4. Emmy
Show answer
Answer: A. Fanny
‘E–G–F’ must be consecutive and B (at an end) touches E, so one end reads B E G F (positions 1–4). D and C are adjacent with C to D's right, and D is third from an end; the only place is positions 5–6 (D is 3rd from the right end). A takes 7. Order: B E G F D C A. The 4th person is F (Fanny). The mirror arrangement (B at the right end) forces D into position 3 with C at 4, which is already taken, so the answer is unique.
Question 2 · Official sample
If A G C I E is written as C I E K G and F L H N J is written as H N J P L, how is L Z P W X B coded?
  1. MAQXYC
  2. MBQXYD
  3. NBRYZD
  4. None of These
Show answer
Answer: C. NBRYZD
A→C, G→I, C→E, I→K, E→G: each letter moves +2. Apply: L→N, Z→B, P→R, W→Y, X→Z, B→D = NBRYZD.
Question 3 · Official sample
If Jenifer's father is the grandfather of Hillary, how is Jenifer's mother related to Hillary?
  1. Sister
  2. Mother
  3. Grandmother
  4. Aunt
Show answer
Answer: C. Grandmother
Jenifer's father is Hillary's grandfather; his wife, Jenifer's mother, is therefore Hillary's grandmother (taking the usual reading that they are the parents of Jenifer). Jenifer herself is Hillary's mother or aunt, so ‘Mother’ and ‘Aunt’ are wrong for Jenifer's mother.
Question 4 · Official sample
In a certain code FRENCH is written as FQEMCG. How is JULY written?
  1. JVLZ
  2. JTLX
  3. IUKY
  4. IULX
Show answer
Answer: B. JTLX
F→F (0), R→Q (−1), E→E (0), N→M (−1), C→C (0), H→G (−1): shifts alternate 0, −1. JULY: J(0)=J, U(−1)=T, L(0)=L, Y(−1)=X → JTLX.
Question 5 · Official sample
Ana starts from point X and walks 5 km west, then turns left and walks 2 km. She again turns left and walks 7 km. In which direction is she from X?
  1. North-west
  2. South-west
  3. South-east
  4. North-east
Show answer
Answer: C. South-east
West 5 → x = −5. Facing west, a left turn points south: 2 km → y = −2. Facing south, a left turn points east: 7 km → x = +2. Final (+2, −2): south-east of X.
Critical Thinking & Verbal Ability
Question 6 · Official sample
Alex bought a phone in country Y. Inserting a SIM gave ‘No sim inserted’. Only Network ABC's SIM worked. Which conclusion can be made?
  1. ‘No sim inserted’ is an issue Alex faced with all networks' SIMs.
  2. All phones sold in country Y have issues.
  3. Network ABC is the best network, which is why its SIM worked.
  4. None of the above.
Show answer
Answer: D. None of the above.
ABC's SIM worked, so the error did not appear with all SIMs (A false). One phone cannot show anything about all phones in Y (B). The facts say nothing about ‘best’ (C). None of the above.
Question 7 · Official sample
‘There are enough problems to speak about, one of them is that people cannot find friends that ideal, but are they themselves the ideal friend.’ Choose the option that correctly rephrases the underlined part (the paper's underlining is not visible in the published PDF).
  1. One of them being that people cannot find ideal friends
  2. One of them is that people cannot find friends which are ideal
  3. One of them is people cannot found friends ideal
  4. And one of them being that people can find friends, but not the ideal
  5. No error
Show answer
Answer: B. One of them is that people cannot find friends which are ideal
Caveat: Krea publishes no key and the underlined portion is not shown, so this is our best judgement. The original joins two independent clauses with a comma (a comma splice) and uses the awkward ‘friends that ideal’. Option B repairs the grammar of the clause without changing its meaning; A leaves a fragment, C has ‘found’ for ‘find’, and D reverses the meaning. Treat B as the most defensible choice, but do not rely on it as an official answer.
Question 8 · Official sample
Identify the error: ‘The amount of hard work put in this project is immense.’
  1. immense
  2. hard work
  3. amount
  4. No error
Show answer
Answer: D. No error
‘Amount’ is correct for an uncountable noun (‘hard work’), and ‘immense’ agrees with it. The only slip is the preposition (‘put into’), which is not one of the options, so of the choices offered the answer is No error. (Krea publishes no key; this is our reading.)
Question 9 · Official sample
Prolonged exposure to ultraviolet rays increases the chances of skin cancer. TV screens, computer monitors and water purifiers also emit UV rays. Which conclusion is best supported?
  1. Prolonged exposure to UV is the primary cause of skin cancer.
  2. TVs, monitors and water purifiers thin the ozone layer.
  3. Certain devices of our day-to-day life can cause skin cancer.
  4. A person with skin cancer must have been exposed to UV from those devices.
Show answer
Answer: C. Certain devices of our day-to-day life can cause skin cancer.
UV raises skin-cancer risk; these devices emit UV; so they can contribute. A over-reaches (‘primary’), B brings in the ozone layer, D says ‘must’. The hedged conclusion C is the only one fully supported.
Question 10 · Official sample
‘If the media and judiciary had not unearthed and corrected the corrupt, the society would have taken corruption as no ______.’
  1. Malaise
  2. Inquest
  3. Dullard
  4. Chortle
Show answer
Answer: A. Malaise
Malaise = a general sense of something being wrong. ‘Taken corruption as no malaise’ = not regarded it as a problem. An inquest is an investigation, a dullard a stupid person, a chortle a chuckle: none fits.
Numeric Reasoning
Question 11 · Official sample
An institute has 720 students. Today 144 are on leave. What percentage are present?
  1. 20%
  2. 51%
  3. 68%
  4. 80%
Show answer
Answer: D. 80%
Present = 720 − 144 = 576; 576/720 × 100 = 80%. (20% is the share on leave.)
Question 12 · Official sample
A pizza costs $50 and a burger $20. Find the ratio of the cost of a burger to that of a pizza.
  1. 2:5
  2. 3:5
  3. 1:5
  4. 4:5
Show answer
Answer: A. 2:5
Burger : pizza = 20 : 50 = 2 : 5 (order matters: burger first).
Question 13 · Official sample
A briefcase is sold at a profit of 10%. If its cost price had been 5% less and it was sold for ₹7 more, the gain would have been 20%. Find the cost price.
  1. ₹175
  2. ₹200
  3. ₹225
  4. ₹160
Show answer
Answer: A. ₹175
Let CP = x. Sold at 1.10x. New CP = 0.95x, new SP = 1.20 × 0.95x = 1.14x. This SP is ₹7 more: 1.14x − 1.10x = 7 → 0.04x = 7 → x = ₹175.
Question 14 · Official sample
What is the ratio of a father's age to his son's age if they are 35 and 5?
  1. 5:1
  2. 6:1
  3. 7:1
  4. 1:7
Show answer
Answer: C. 7:1
35 : 5 = 7 : 1.
Question 15 · Official sample
UCUCU Bank's revenues are 20% more than Lena Bank's. By what percent are Lena Bank's revenues less than UCUCU's?
  1. 22.5%
  2. 20%
  3. 25%
  4. 16.67%
Show answer
Answer: D. 16.67%
Lena = 100, UCUCU = 120. Lena is less by 20/120 × 100 = 16.67% – the base is now UCUCU's revenue.
