Critical Thinking & Verbal Ability

Grammar, vocabulary in context, conclusions, assumptions, flaws, strengthen/weaken, sentence order. 26 practice questions with worked answers. These are separate from the three online tests.

Question 1
A bakery's sales rose 20% this year, yet its profits fell.
Which statement best resolves the apparent paradox?
  1. Sales rose mainly around festivals.
  2. Customers prefer fresh bread.
  3. The cost of ingredients rose faster than the bakery's prices.
  4. The bakery opened an hour earlier.
Show answer
Answer: C. The cost of ingredients rose faster than the bakery's prices.
If costs rise faster than revenue, higher sales can still shrink profit.
Question 2
“Every player in the team is excellent, so the team is excellent.”
The reasoning is flawed because it assumes:
  1. the team has no weak player.
  2. all teams have excellent players.
  3. excellence cannot be measured.
  4. what is true of each part must be true of the whole.
Show answer
Answer: D. what is true of each part must be true of the whole.
Fallacy of composition: a team also depends on coordination, not just individual skill.
Question 3
Choose the best option to complete: Unless you ______ harder, you will not pass.
  1. worked not
  2. will work
  3. work
  4. do not work
Show answer
Answer: C. work
‘Unless’ already carries the negative, so the verb is positive; present tense in a first conditional.

Third conditional: If I had known, I would have told you.
Question 5

Question 8
Choose the word that best completes the sentence.
The judge's verdict was ______; no one could accuse her of favouring either side.

Show answer
Answer: D. impartial

Question 9
In 2020, 40% of households in Town A owned a car; in 2025, 52% did.
Which conclusion is valid?
  1. Public transport use fell.
  2. The town's population grew.
  3. The share of car-owning households rose by 12 percentage points.
  4. The number of cars rose by 30%.
Show answer
Answer: C. The share of car-owning households rose by 12 percentage points.
52 − 40 = 12 percentage points. A rise from 40% to 52% is 30% in relative terms but that is about the share, not the number of cars; population data is absent.

Question 13
“Our school should buy tablets for every student, because students who own tablets score higher.”
What is the key assumption?
  1. Tablets help raise scores, rather than high scorers simply being more likely to own tablets.
  2. All schools can afford tablets.
  3. Tablets cost less than books.
  4. Students dislike printed books.
Show answer
Answer: A. Tablets help raise scores, rather than high scorers simply being more likely to own tablets.
The argument moves from correlation to cause; it assumes the causal direction.
Question 14
No reptile is warm-blooded. All snakes are reptiles.
Which must be true?
  1. All reptiles are snakes.
  2. No snake is warm-blooded.
  3. Some warm-blooded animals are reptiles.
  4. Some snakes are warm-blooded.
Show answer
Answer: B. No snake is warm-blooded.
Snakes ⊂ reptiles, and reptiles have no overlap with warm-blooded animals, so neither do snakes.
Question 15
Statement: ‘Ban plastic bags in the city.’
Which argument is strongest in favour?
  1. Many people use plastic bags.
  2. Plastic bags are cheap.
  3. Other cities allow plastic bags.
  4. Plastic bags clog drains and cause flooding in the city.
Show answer
Answer: D. Plastic bags clog drains and cause flooding in the city.
A strong argument is relevant and specific to the proposal's goal (harm to the city).
Question 16

The news about the floods were shocking.
  1. about the floods
  2. The news
  3. No error
  4. were shocking
Show answer
Answer: D. were shocking
‘News’ is an uncountable singular noun: was shocking.
Question 17
All shops on Park Road close by 9 pm. Some shops on Park Road sell electronics.
Which conclusion is best supported?
  1. Shops outside Park Road stay open late.
  2. All electronics shops in the city close by 9 pm.
  3. No shop on Park Road is open after 8 pm.
  4. Some shops that sell electronics close by 9 pm.
Show answer
Answer: D. Some shops that sell electronics close by 9 pm.
Electronics shops on Park Road are Park Road shops, and all of those close by 9 pm. The rest go beyond the information.
Question 18
Choose the option that best captures the main idea.
Cities that plant trees along roads see cooler summers, cleaner air and calmer traffic. Yet most councils cut tree-planting budgets first.
  1. Trees are expensive to maintain.
  2. Traffic is the main problem of cities.
  3. Councils should stop cutting budgets.
  4. Tree-planting has several benefits but is undervalued in budgets.
Show answer
Answer: D. Tree-planting has several benefits but is undervalued in budgets.
The passage lists benefits and then the contrasting neglect; the best summary joins the two.

Question 24
Choose the word that best completes the sentence.
Scientists must remain ______; they should not accept a claim without evidence.
  1. complacent
  2. credulous
  3. sceptical
  4. gullible
Show answer
Answer: C. sceptical
Sceptical = demanding evidence. Credulous and gullible mean the opposite.
Question 25
Only members can borrow books from the club library. Meera borrowed a book.
What follows?
  1. Meera is the librarian.
  2. All members borrow books.
  3. Meera is a member.
  4. Meera borrowed more than one book.
Show answer
Answer: C. Meera is a member.
Borrowing requires membership; the converse (all members borrow) does not follow.
Question 26
Choose the sentence that is grammatically correct.
  1. The principal, along with the teachers, were planning a trip.
  2. The principal, along with the teachers, are planning a trip.
  3. The principal, along with the teachers, is planning a trip.
  4. The principal, along with the teachers, planning a trip is.
Show answer
Answer: C. The principal, along with the teachers, is planning a trip.
‘Along with’ phrases do not change the number of the subject; the subject is the singular ‘principal’.
